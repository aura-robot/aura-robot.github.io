AuRA: From Atoms to Bits
through Autonomous Robot Interaction

Xinchen Yu1*, Yoonkyo Jung1*, Haoran Chen1, Zikui Cai1, Aayush Talreja1,
Seungjae Lee1, Hyunjin Kim1, Furong Huang1,2
1University of Maryland, College Park    2All Purpose AI

Abstract

Simulation enables robot-learning experiments at scale, but acquiring physics-informed digital twins often requires human manipulation and manual annotation of recorded interactions. To reduce this reliance on human effort, we present AuRA, a system that automates robot interaction and data preparation for digital-twin reconstruction. AuRA combines execution-aware grasp selection, gripper-motion measurements derived from robot states, and registered pre-interaction geometry and appearance priors. These choices enable autonomous collection, avoid reliance on visual gripper tracking, and recover object information obscured by the robot.

Across 13 deformable objects, AuRA reduces active human labor and wall-clock collection time per successfully reconstructed asset by 88.5% and 55.4%, respectively. Relative to the human-collected PhysTwin baseline, AuRA improves seven of nine metrics for future prediction under known gripper motion, including a 28.1% reduction in one-way L1 Chamfer distance. It also achieves similar mean silhouette IoU and Chamfer distance under the evaluated unseen interactions. These results highlight the potential of autonomous robot interaction to support more efficient digital-twin acquisition with comparable reconstruction quality.

Approach

Replacing the human operator with a robot raises three coupled challenges. AuRA answers each with one design choice, on top of the PhysTwin reconstruction backend.

1 · Interaction

Execution-aware grasp selection

A visually plausible grasp may violate joint limits or collide with the table or cameras.

AnyGrasp candidates from a multi-view RGB-D scan are checked for tabletop clearance and joint-limit margins along the planned motion. The robot executes the best feasible grasp-and-lift while recording RGB-D videos and robot states.

2 · Motion

Controller trajectories from robot states

Visually estimated gripper trajectories suffer from segmentation, tracking, and depth errors.

Recorded joint states and calibrated gripper geometry give the paths of gripper-attached controller points directly, even when the gripper is occluded. Visual tracking is used only for the object's response.

3 · Occlusion

Pre-interaction priors

The gripper and arm hide parts of the object's geometry and appearance.

A static point cloud and a Gaussian appearance model built from the pre-interaction scan are registered to the first interaction frame and fill in the regions the robot obscures.

Results

We evaluate on 13 deformable objects with a Franka robot arm and three RGB-D cameras, comparing against human-operated collection with the PhysTwin pipeline by operators in three experience groups (amateur, trained, expert).

AuRA produces 26 reconstructed assets from 35 acquisition attempts (74.3%), whereas human-operated collection produces 21 from 78 attempts overall (26.9%). Per reconstructed asset, AuRA needs 126.7 s of wall-clock time and only 32.7 s of human labor, since a person is needed only to place the object and start the system, compared with 284.4 s for human collection overall.

Collection costs exclude data processing, reconstruction training, and untimed backup trials.

2. Reconstruction Quality

Resimulation and future prediction against the human-collected PhysTwin baseline

Following PhysTwin, the first 70% of each interaction is used for training and the remaining 30% for testing under known controller motion. AuRA improves seven of nine future-prediction metrics, with clear gains in silhouette and geometry: the one-way L1 Chamfer distance drops by 28.1% and depth coverage rises by 9.9 points. PhysTwin, which retains about 2.4× more tracking points, fits the training frames more closely.

3. Unseen Interactions

Reconstructed assets driven by new interactions

For each object, one asset per method is driven by a new, teleoperated drag-and-lift interaction. AuRA with static Gaussians and PhysTwin reach similar mean performance (mask IoU 0.3861 vs. 0.3849, one-way Chamfer 43.554 mm vs. 43.430 mm, PSNR 10.379 dB vs. 10.309 dB). Static Gaussians trained on the pre-interaction scan also beat first-frame Gaussians on every appearance and silhouette metric, because they keep the appearance of regions the robot later covers.

4. Ablations

Controller trajectory source, static geometry prior, and object tracker

  • Controller trajectory source. On the same 26 robot-collected trajectories, controllers derived from robot states enable successful reconstruction in 26/26 cases (100.0%), compared with 2/26 (7.7%) for controllers obtained through visual tracking, depth lifting, and PhysTwin-style filtering.
  • Static geometry prior. Adding the registered pre-interaction point cloud improves most metrics for every object tracker tested (CoTracker3, MVTracker, MV-TAP).
  • Object tracker. Among point-cloud-initialized trackers, MV-TAP generally performs best in both resimulation and future prediction.
